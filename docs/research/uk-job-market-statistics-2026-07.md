## Classification and revision notes

- The ONS currently labels the LFS-derived employment, unemployment and inactivity estimates as **official statistics in development**, not accredited official statistics.
- Vacancy Survey estimates are **accredited official statistics**. The latest three-month vacancy figure is an early estimate and can be revised.
- The unemployed-to-vacancy ratio combines accredited vacancy statistics with an LFS input whose development status and uncertainty still apply.
- The July 2026 release revised seasonally adjusted LFS estimates back to January to March 2020 after an annual review. Historical values in the article come from the revised July datasets.
- The June 2026 Claimant Count is provisional and subject to later administrative revision.
- ISE results are industry survey evidence from participating employers. They are clearly separated from national official statistics.
- Totaljobs results are current industry survey evidence. They support reported candidate and recruiter friction but do not establish a national applications-per-person requirement or prove a single cause of longer hiring times.

## Rejected or unverified claims

| Rejected claim | Decision and reason |
| --- | --- |
| “It takes 27 applications to receive an interview.” | Rejected. No original, current UK study with an assessable sample and methodology was located. Employer-side graduate application ratios cannot be converted into applications required by one person. |
| “It takes 162 applications to receive a job offer.” | Rejected for the same reason: no verified original current UK evidence, and no defensible conversion from employer applications received to individual outcomes. |
| “Only 4% of applicants get an interview.” | Rejected. The denominator, sector, recruitment stage, time period and original study were not established. |
| “Only 1% get the job.” | Rejected. The population, denominator and original methodology were not established; it would falsely imply a universal success rate. |
| “Every UK job receives 280 applications.” | Rejected. No national source supports this, and graduate-programme evidence cannot be generalised to every occupation or vacancy. |
| “The average person must make 100 applications before finding work.” | Rejected. No credible current UK person-level source supports a universal requirement, and individual outcomes vary materially. |
| “AI is the primary cause of present unemployment or falling vacancies.” | Rejected. The reviewed ONS releases measure outcomes but do not attribute them to AI. ISE identifies several possible factors within graduate recruitment, including technology, wider eligibility, reduced hiring and economic pressure; it does not establish a single national cause. |
